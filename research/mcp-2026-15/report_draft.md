# MCP 2026-15: Market Research, Rotary-Wing Aircraft SIN

Draft for insertion into the MCP business case. Status as of October 19, 2026: federal contract data (USAspending, FY2016 to FY2026) is in and analyzed. Still pending: SRP sales on GS-07F-139DA before FY2025 and by SIN and customer type, FAIRS fleet data, LESO/1033 holdings, and the document that created the temporary accommodation. Cells marked **[PENDING]** wait on those.

The aircraft-purchase figures come from `civilian_aircraft_purchases.csv`, a hand-tagged list of every civilian award for a complete helicopter. Sustainment, parts, overhaul, and the Coast Guard's H-60 hull rebuild are excluded. Each row cites the FPDS award.

## Part 1. Facts and Figures (leadership sheet)

### What changed between September 2025 and May 2026

Nothing changed in how CBP buys. Every CBP Light Enforcement Helicopter purchase since 2018 has been a MAS order to Davenport Aviation under GS-07F-139DA: five orders, 42 aircraft, $323 million. GSS recommended moving off MAS a customer that had used nothing else for seven years.

The rules changed instead. MAS Refresh 26 (SF30 dated April 25, 2025, Mass Mod A897) wrote into SIN 336413 that "the award of rotary-wing or fixed-wing aircraft is explicitly excluded." The GSS recommendation followed that rule. CBP then ordered aircraft on GS-07F-139DA twice after the exclusion took effect: two helicopters for $17.6 million on September 9, 2025 (modification P00004 to order 70B02C23F00000959), and ten for $95.5 million on May 19, 2026 (order 70B02C26F00000290). GSA's September 30, 2026 RFI describes helicopters as "currently accommodated on a temporary basis under an existing Security and Protection SIN."

The document that created that accommodation, and its effective date, is now the most important item in the file. If it postdates either order, $113 million in aircraft was ordered against a SIN whose text excluded them. The business case should answer that before a protester or the IG asks.

Purchases are attributed to the fiscal year each order was awarded. Three years had none, and the largest year was more than three times the median. Any projection built on a single year will be wrong.

The draft package's "about 70 percent" CBP share reproduces on this base. CBP bought 42 of the 60 aircraft whose counts FPDS states (70 percent). Three buys (Park Police, DEA's used Bell 407s, and a 2016 APHIS purchase) don't state a count; estimating them puts CBP at 63 to 64 percent of aircraft, the same as its dollar share. The business case should say "about two-thirds."

The broader PSC 1520 totals in `tables.md` ($1.34 billion civilian) are not the market. Most of that is Coast Guard MH-60T and MH-65 sustainment and parts, plus a $315 million Sikorsky contract for new H-60 hulls in the MH-60T service-life extension. None of it is a commercial aircraft purchase that a SIN could carry.

#### 1.2 State and local demand

The GSS memo called state and local demand "limited." The record of how this channel began says otherwise. GSA added the H125 to the schedule in 2017, under SIN 567-99 on legacy Schedule 84, after the Mississippi Department of Public Safety and the Hillsborough County Sheriff's Office asked for an expedited award (Federal Times, March 2017). State and local buyers created the channel; CBP's first order came a year later.

By January 2020, GSA Region 7 reported 23 helicopters ordered through it "from a variety of federal, state and local customers." FPDS shows 19 federal aircraft ordered by then (7 CBP in July 2018, 9 CBP and 3 NASA in August and September 2019), so roughly four of the 23 were state and local, Albuquerque's among them. Known state and local orders on GS-07F-139DA since:

- Baltimore City: $17,926,923 for police helicopters (Board of Estimates, December 2021)
- Albuquerque PD: two H125s (first January 2020 via Schedule 84 Cooperative Purchasing)
- New Mexico State Police: H125 (November 2025)
- Baltimore County PD: second H125, replacing an AS350B3 (August 2026)
- Phoenix PD: H145 (date not confirmed)

Reported schedule sales (SSQ+, GS-07F-139DA, all SINs) cover only FY2025 Q1 through FY2026 Q2 so far:

| | FY25 Q1 | FY25 Q2 | FY25 Q3 | FY25 Q4 | FY26 Q1 | FY26 Q2 | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Reported sales ($M) | 4.13 | 6.70 | 0.00 | 0.51 | 3.88 | 34.89 | 50.11 |

Sales are reported when invoiced, usually at aircraft delivery, so they lag FPDS orders by a year or more and can't be matched quarter by quarter. This view doesn't split federal from state and local. SSQ+ lists the contract under the Industrial Products and Services category (contracting office 47QSMS), not Security and Protection. That needs reconciling with the RFI's statement that helicopters sit on a Security and Protection SIN. A SIN-level breakdown and a longer timeframe are **[PENDING]**. FPDS does not record non-federal orders.

"Limited" is partly right for a reason GSS did not identify: much state and local rotary-wing demand never reaches a commercial market. The DoD 1033 program had distributed 616 aircraft to law enforcement agencies since 2006, most of them helicopters (Vertical, about 2015); Florida alone held almost 100. They are loaned with title retained by DLA, for a service fee (Citrus County, Florida took four OH-58s for $12,000). That pool is aging out. OH-58s and UH-1s built in the late 1960s and 1970s are now 50 years old or more, and the replacement path visible in the press is commercial: San Juan County, New Mexico replaced a 1033 OH-58 with a new Bell 505. LESO holdings by type and transfer year are **[PENDING]**.

Federal grants are not a visible funding source. The USAspending assistance pull (Byrne JAG and Homeland Security Grant Program, aircraft keywords) returned 19 transactions totaling $1.1 million, all operating support, drones, or equipment; none funded an aircraft purchase. Homeland Security Grant Program money passes through states and is not described at the prime-award level, so grant-funded purchases may exist without appearing here.

State and local fleet size: the most rigorous national figure is old. BJS counted 201 aviation units and 604 helicopters in agencies with 100 or more sworn officers in 2007. APSA outreach should produce a current count.

#### 1.3 DoD

DoD obligated about $75 billion under PSC 1520 and NAICS 336411 helicopter descriptions in FY16-FY26, almost all of it program aircraft (UH-60M, MH-60R, CH-47, UH-1Y, TH-73) and their sustainment. It is out of scope for the SIN. DoD's open-market buys of commercial models (Bell 407GX, AW119Kx, MD 530F, Bell 505) are largely foreign military sales and show only that OEMs sell direct when the lot justifies it. DoD placed $7.4 million in MAS orders with helicopter descriptions over the decade, none identified as aircraft purchases.

### 2. Industry Analysis

#### 2.1 Who sells

| Seller type | Civilian purchase dollars, FY16-FY26 | Who |
|---|---|---|
| Authorized dealer on MAS | $346M (68%) | Davenport Aviation, Airbus only |
| OEM direct | $150M (29%) | Bell (FBI, DEA, Park Police, WAPA), Leonardo (NNSA) |
| Other dealers and used-aircraft sellers | $13M (3%) | Rainier Heli, Shier Aviation, Trinity Aviation, others |

One woman-owned small business carries the entire schedule channel. GS-07F-139DA is a broad contract (FPDS NAICS 339113 on its orders; it has also sold COVID test kits to GSA, BOP, and Navy) rather than an aviation contract, which is part of why the aircraft sit awkwardly under a Security and Protection SIN. Helicopter Services, Inc. (47QMCA20D001T, SIN 336413) is the only other MAS holder found with aircraft-related SINs, and it shows $4,259 in helicopter-coded federal sales over the decade. Full eLibrary confirmation is **[PENDING]**.

The OEM side explains the dealer model. GAMA reports 732 turbine helicopter deliveries worldwide in 2025 (preliminary), valued at about $4.7 billion. Federal civilian purchases average $46 million a year. That isn't enough for an OEM to carry a schedule contract, which is why Airbus sells to CBP through a dealer and to the Army through program contracts.

Bell is the gap. It won $114 million of the $187 million non-CBP civilian market, all off-schedule. A new SIN grows beyond CBP only if Bell or a Bell dealer gets on it.

#### 2.2 Competition

| Vehicle (civilian purchases) | Awards | Average offers | Single-offer |
|---|---|---|---|
| MAS orders (Davenport) | 7 | 1.1 | 6 of 7 |
| Agency IDIQ orders (FBI, DEA with Bell) | 7 | not reported at order level | |
| Open market | 9 | 3.2 | 3 of 9 |

CBP's orders are competed on paper and draw one offer. The FY2026 order drew two. The open-market buys draw more offers, but they are not comparable: most are used or lightly equipped aircraft from any manufacturer, while CBP buys new, configured H125s on a brand-name basis to keep its fleet common. The data does not support the GSS claim that open-market competition among Airbus resellers would beat MAS pricing. No open-market buy of a configured H125 exists to compare against. The direct test is the eBuy record for the FY2026 order: who the second offeror was, and how far apart the two prices were.

FPDS does not code brand-name justifications for schedule orders in any field the pull captured. The limited-sources justifications posted with each CBP RFQ under FAR 8.405-6 are the record and should be pulled from eBuy.

#### 2.3 Other channels for state and local buyers

No evidence found that Sourcewell, NASPO ValuePoint, or HGACBuy carries complete helicopters; confirm on each portal before the BCA asserts there is no competing cooperative channel. The 1122 program is a second legal route onto the schedule for counter-drug, homeland security, and emergency response purchases.

|---|---|

| Repair and maintenance (J01x) | 365 |

DoD adds $8.1 billion in parts, $3.7 billion in repair, and $624 million in services. The Coast Guard bought 97 percent of civilian helicopter parts ($553 million, mostly PSC 1615 airframe components for the MH-60T and MH-65), and its annual parts spend tripled from $28 million in FY2016 to $94 million in FY2026 as those fleets aged. Coast Guard and NASA are also the largest civilian buyers of repair and services. CBP shows only $1.5 million in keyword-matched parts, so its H125 support likely runs through vehicles this pull did not capture. All of these are floors: each pull required a helicopter keyword, and Forest Service and Interior fire aviation, usually described by mission rather than aircraft type, is barely captured ($9 million for Forest Service).

Civilian agencies spent at least twice as much keeping helicopters flying ($1.09 billion) as buying them ($510 million). An aircraft-purchase SIN addresses at most a third of civilian rotary-wing contract spend. Parts and repair are already reachable through existing MAS SINs and agency sustainment contracts, and the business case should not count them as SIN volume.

### 3. Analysis of Alternatives

**A. Remove helicopters from MAS (GSS, September 2025).** This moves 68 percent of federal civilian helicopter purchase dollars off the schedule, including the only customer that has bought through MAS every year it bought. CBP would need a standalone competition for each order, and the brand-name justification it now posts on eBuy becomes a J&A under FAR 6.302. State and local buyers lose their only federal channel. Cooperative Purchasing authority (40 U.S.C. 502(c)(1)(B)) reaches "law enforcement and security equipment" in the legacy FSC 84 group; it does not reach agency open-market contracts. The data gives no evidence that open-market pricing would be lower.

**B. Status quo (temporary accommodation).** Keeps the channel open but rests on an accommodation that contradicts SIN 336413's text. Two CBP aircraft orders ($113 million) were placed after the exclusion. Every further order carries scope risk until the accommodation is documented with an effective date.

**C. Dedicated rotary-wing SIN (MCP 2026-15).** Formalizes the channel CBP already uses. Three conditions decide whether it works:

1. *Configuration in scope.* Configuration is about a third of CBP's price per aircraft ($7.19M configured vs. $4.77M lightly equipped, 2022). The draft RFI's Phase 1 exclusion of mission-system integration would leave the anchor customer unable to place its next order. Allow the SIN to cover a complete aircraft as delivered, including mission equipment installed before delivery; put training devices (CBP bought two simulators for $5.0 million), spares, and program management on the Ancillary SIN.
2. *Cooperative Purchasing eligibility.* The SIN must sit where 40 U.S.C. 502(c)(1)(B) reaches, or the state and local market closes. Get an OGC opinion before the SIN text is final.
3. *A second manufacturer.* Without Bell or a Bell dealer, the SIN is a single-dealer, single-customer vehicle: CBP plus a few Airbus buyers. Outreach to Bell dealers should be part of the launch plan, since Bell won 61 percent of non-CBP civilian purchase dollars off-schedule.

**Recommendation: C, conditioned on all three.** The data does not support A. B is not sustainable.

## Method and limits

- Source: USAspending prime transactions, PSC 1520, NAICS 336411 with helicopter descriptions, PSC 1510/1560 with helicopter keywords, and every order under GS-07F-139DA. Pulled October 19, 2026. FY2026 is incomplete in FPDS; DoD runs about 90 days behind.
- Aircraft purchases were tagged by hand from award descriptions. Three purchases lack an aircraft count and are shown as estimates.
- Customer is the funding agency, so CBP aircraft bought through Army contracts would count as CBP. None appeared in the purchase data.
- Adjacent spend is keyword-limited and understates fire aviation. The civilian/DoD split for parts uses funding agency name ("Defense" = DoD).
