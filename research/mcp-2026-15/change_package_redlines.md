# MCP 2026-15: Redlines from the FPDS and SSQ+ Data

Section-by-section changes to the completed draft change package ("New Helicopter SIN Completed Draft"), with notes on the September 2025 GSS market research and the draft RFI. Every figure traces to `civilian_aircraft_purchases.csv`, the GS-07F-139DA order file, or SSQ+ sales for GS-07F-139DA (FY2017 Q2 to FY2026 Q2).

The package is in better shape than the Grants QSMO template it replaced. Its structure, eligibility criteria, Ordering Guide, and risk plan stand. What needs to change is the demand math, the CBP order history, one scope boundary, and the maximum order. The data also gives the package stronger answers to the GSS memo than it currently uses.

---

## 1. Executive Summary, Background

**Current:** "Helicopters were previously available through the MAS Program but were removed during earlier efforts to eliminate low-demand SINs."

**Problem:** Helicopters were never actually off MAS. Davenport reported $293.9 million in sales on GS-07F-139DA from FY2020 through FY2026 Q2, including $39.3 million after Refresh 26 excluded complete aircraft from SIN 336413. "Low-demand" doesn't describe a contract averaging $45 million a year.

**Replace with:** "Refresh 26 (April 2025) excluded complete aircraft from SIN 336413 as part of the MAS rightsizing effort. Helicopters nonetheless continued to move through the schedule: CBP ordered two aircraft in September 2025 and ten in May 2026, and the existing reseller reported $39.3 million in helicopter sales after the exclusion took effect. GSA now accommodates rotary-wing aircraft on a temporary basis under SIN 336413. This package replaces that accommodation with a SIN written for complete aircraft."

## 2. Brief Summary, Maximum Order

**Current:** "$10,000,000 (proposed; sized so a single configured light or medium helicopter falls under the threshold)."

**Problem:** CBP's configured H125 was already $9.55 million in May 2026 and has risen about 5 percent a year since 2019 ($6.57M → $9.55M). It will cross $10 million on the next order. A configured medium twin (NNSA paid $18.0 million per Leonardo in FY2022) is well above it.

**Replace with:** "$15,000,000 (proposed; a configured light single is $9.55 million at May 2026 prices and rising about 5 percent a year; a configured medium twin ran about $18 million in FY2022)." Alternatively, keep $10 million and state plainly that orders above it are expected and handled by FAR 8.405-4 price-reduction requests.

## 3. SIN Instructions, Out of Scope (third-party mission-system integration)

**Current:** Third-party mission-system integration and STC completion work, "including sensors, hoists, medical interiors, and law enforcement equipment," is deferred. Other Considerations asks CBP to confirm whether its equipment is OEM-installed.

**What FPDS shows:** CBP's configuration includes third-party equipment. Order 70B02C23F00000959 carries a $217,964 modification for a "FLIR 380 HD upgrade to a FLIR 380X" (April 2025) and a $135,000 modification for "Troll switch integration" (April 2026). FLIR (Teledyne) and Troll Systems are third-party mission-equipment suppliers, not Airbus catalog options. Configuration accounts for about a third of CBP's per-aircraft price: Fish and Wildlife paid $4.77 million for a lightly equipped H125 on the same contract in May 2022, against CBP's $7.19 million configured price four months later.

**Recommendation:** As drafted, Phase 1 cannot carry CBP's next order. Either (a) bring dealer-installed mission equipment delivered as part of the aircraft into Phase 1 scope, priced as separately listed options, with the STC holder identified; or (b) keep the exclusion and say directly that CBP's configured buy would have to be split across vehicles. Option (a) is the only version under which the anchor customer uses the SIN.

## 7. Demand projection (assumptions and table)

**Current assumptions:** federal civilian demand of 10 to 15 helicopters a year, $100 million midpoint; SIN capture rising from 40 to 65 percent; state and local $10 million rising to $30 million. Year 1 total $50 million, year 5 $103 million, IFF $375,000 to $774,000.

**Problems:**
- Federal civilian purchases averaged $46 million a year (median $45 million) and about 5.5 aircraft a year, not $100 million and 10 to 15. The inventory method roughly doubles demand because agencies replace owned aircraft more slowly than a 20-25 year cycle implies and contract much of their flying.
- MAS already captures 68 percent of federal civilian purchase dollars. A capture rate starting at 40 percent implies the SIN would lose volume relative to today.
- Actual combined sales on the existing contract averaged about $45 million a year (FY2020 to FY2026 Q2), producing about $340,000 a year in IFF ($2.2 million total).

**Replace the table with one built on actuals:**

| Year | Federal through SIN | State/local through SIN | Total | IFF (0.75%) | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | $31M | $14M | $45M | $340K | FY2020-FY2026 average on the existing contract |
| 2 | $33M | $15M | $48M | $360K | 5% price growth, no new buyers |
| 3 | $38M | $15M | $53M | $400K | A Bell or second Airbus dealer awarded; one-third of Bell's ~$10M/yr civilian volume moves to MAS |
| 4 | $40M | $16M | $56M | $420K | Same |
| 5 | $42M | $17M | $59M | $440K | Same |

Keep the existing caution that volume arrives in lumps: FY2026 MAS federal obligations were $98.6 million; FY2016 and FY2017 were zero and FY2020 was $6.6 million.

**Also revise** the Financial Analysis bullet ("$50 million in year one rising to about $103 million by year five ... $375,000 to $774,000") to match the table, and the Appendix Market Demand Analysis, which can drop the inventory-based estimate entirely.

## 8. Stakeholder concerns, answering the GSS memo

The package's three answers are sound. The data makes each one stronger:

- **Lack of common spend.** Add: "Federal civilian demand is $46 million a year, concentrated in CBP, but the existing channel also carries $10 million to $17 million a year in state and local sales. Combined volume of about $45 million a year has generated $2.2 million in fee since FY2020."
- **MAS does not create competition.** Add: "Four of CBP's five MAS orders drew one offer; the May 2026 order drew two. The single-offer pattern follows from CBP's brand-name, small-business set-aside requirement and the one authorized reseller on schedule, not from MAS. Non-CBP civilian buyers who went open market drew 3.2 offers on average, but they were buying used or lightly equipped aircraft of any make, not configured H125s, so the comparison does not show open market would price CBP's requirement lower. No open-market purchase of a configured H125 exists in FPDS to test the GSS claim."
- **GSA lacks internal expertise.** No change.

Add one fact to "Since that research, the facts have moved": CBP placed an order for two aircraft on GS-07F-139DA on September 9, 2025, the same month the GSS memo recommended removal.

The GSS memo also says CBP "previously sourced helicopters directly from Airbus." FPDS for FY2016 through FY2026 shows no CBP aircraft purchase from Airbus directly; every CBP helicopter purchase in that window went through Davenport on the schedule. If the memo meant buys before FY2016, the package should say so; otherwise the statement should not be carried forward.

## 9. Other Considerations (temporary accommodation)

**Current:** The PMO should "document how complete helicopters came to be accommodated on a temporary basis after Refresh 26."

**Add:** "SSQ+ shows the existing reseller reported helicopter sales under three SINs: legacy SIN 567-99 (FY2020), SIN 332216, Law Enforcement, Firefighting and Rescue Tools, Equipment and Accessories ($84.5 million, FY2020 Q4 to FY2022 Q1), and SIN 336413 ($201 million from FY2020 Q4). $39.3 million of the 336413 sales were reported after the April 2025 exclusion, and CBP orders totaling $113 million were placed on September 9, 2025 and May 19, 2026. The effective date of the temporary accommodation determines whether those orders were within the contract's scope at award. The authorizing document was not located during market research; the PMO should document it before the package is approved. Neither SIN 332216 nor SIN 336413 was written for complete aircraft, and SSQ+ files the contract under Industrial Products and Services rather than Security and Protection."

## The draft RFI

The RFI closed October 14, 2026, so its text no longer matters; how its responses are read does.

- Section 2 says agencies "generally acquire them through agency-specific contracts ... rather than a shared MAS product listing." For federal civilian purchases that is backwards: 68 percent went through MAS.
- The Phase 1 exclusion of mission-system integration (Section 3) is the boundary in redline 3. Read responses to question 7 for whether dealers price installed mission equipment as options on the aircraft. If they do, that supports bringing it into Phase 1.
- Question 6 (used and demonstrator aircraft): non-CBP agencies bought $14.6 million in used aircraft over the decade (DEA, DOE/WAPA, State, and probably NPS). Excluding used aircraft is defensible, but it does cost the SIN that volume.
